Co-operator and editor of JAPAN'S BEST WAGYU, an independent selection of Japan's best wagyu and yakiniku restaurants, chaired by Takayuki Toriyama of Wagyu Journal. I run the English edition, the underlying data, and the technical side.

What I actually doやっていること

The English-language internet gets wagyu wrong in a specific and measurable way. Retail sites still report that only 1–2% of Japanese cattle earn A5. Japan's own grading authority puts the figure at 76.6% of graded steer wagyu as of May 2026.

My job is the unglamorous half of fixing that: going to the primary sources, doing the arithmetic, writing it in English, and building it so that search engines and AI systems can actually read it.

  • Primary data. I compiled thirty years of Japan Meat Grading Association results (1996–2026) into a single English series — something that did not previously exist in any language in assembled form.
  • The English edition. Editing and publishing the knowledge articles, the standard we judge by, and the restaurant selection in English.
  • Accuracy. Fact-checking every figure against government and industry sources before publication, and correcting the record where English-language sources have it wrong.
  • The technical layer. Structured data, indexing, and making the work legible to AI systems — the part that decides whether accurate information is ever seen.

The selection itself is not mine. It is made by a committee that dines unannounced, pays its own way, and takes no money from any restaurant, producer or company it covers. I run the publication, not the verdict.
